About me Teaching

I am a Ph.D. candidate at the Research Center for Social Complexity (CICS), where my research explores social contagion and diffusion processes in networks using computational and quantitative approaches.

With a background in Physics and Social Sciences (B.Sc. 2020, M.Sc. 2022, UdeC), my work bridges theoretical modeling and empirical applications—from epidemiological simulations to the study of social influence.

I contribute to netdiffuseR, an R package for the analysis of diffusion and contagion processes on networks, mostly on features for epidemiological data and contact-game datasets. I have taught it as an instructor at workshops at USC and at the Sunbelt conference.

In 2026 I was a visiting researcher at the University of Southern California, working with Thomas W. Valente on the disadoption of behaviors in social networks.

My current projects, funded by ANID (FONDECYT), apply network models—such as Ising models—to recidivism risk among incarcerated populations and run analyses of political dynamics in the Chilean Constitutional Convention.

I am also interested in teaching, from graduate-level courses and workshops to curricular materials for undergraduate education. You can find them under Teaching.

You can find my code and projects on GitHub, or contact me at an.oliveram@udd.cl.
